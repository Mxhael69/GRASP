Food Preservation

Food preservation techniques help slow down chemical reactions and the growth of microorganisms that cause food spoilage.

❄️ Refrigeration

Refrigeration lowers the temperature of food, slowing down the growth of microorganisms.

Reaction Rate: Slower

Lower temperatures reduce the rate of many chemical reactions and slow bacterial growth.

🧊 Freezing

Freezing turns much of the water in food into ice, greatly slowing microbial activity.

Reaction Rate: Very Slow

The low temperature significantly slows chemical reactions and prevents most microorganisms from actively multiplying.

☀️ Drying

Drying removes moisture from food, making it harder for microorganisms to grow.

Reaction Rate: Slower

Removing water reduces the conditions needed for microorganisms and some chemical reactions to occur.

🧂 Salting

Salt draws water away from food and microorganisms, helping prevent spoilage.

Reaction Rate: Slower

Salt reduces available water, which makes it difficult for many microorganisms to grow.

🥫 Canning

Canning uses heat treatment and sealed containers to help prevent microorganisms from contaminating food.

Reaction Rate: Reduced

Heat treatment can destroy many microorganisms, while sealing prevents new contamination.

WHY FOOD PRESERVATION MATTERS

The Importance of Food Preservation

Food preservation is important because it helps extend the shelf life of food and slows down the processes that cause food to spoil. By using different preservation techniques, food can remain safe, useful, and suitable for consumption for a longer period of time.

Preservation also helps reduce food waste by allowing excess food to be stored and used later. Understanding how preservation techniques affect reaction rates allows us to see how science can be applied to our everyday lives.
